Sharing the park

Heʻeia State Park is a public park and stays open to visitors until 5:00 pm. Before then you may share the grounds and the parking lot with other visitors.

Parking

  • The main lot has about 70 marked stalls, and there is more parking in the front lot.
  • Please don’t park on the grass or block the driveway. The driveway is an emergency lane, and the park tows cars that park on the grass or block it.
  • When the lots are full, there is no other parking in the park, so please carpool if you can.

Weather

Windward Oʻahu often gets passing showers and steady trade winds, even on sunny days. Showers tend to pass quickly. Once the sun drops behind the mountains, the breeze can feel cool, so bring a light layer for the evening.

Travel and stay

It’s Labor Day weekend

The wedding is on the Sunday of Labor Day weekend, and Monday, September 6 is the holiday. Flights, hotels, and rental cars on Oʻahu fill up and get expensive, so please book early.

Flying in

Fly into Daniel K. Inouye International Airport (HNL) in Honolulu, the main airport on Oʻahu.

Getting to the venue

  • From the airport: about 30 to 45 minutes by car, over the Koʻolau mountains on the H-3 freeway or the Likelike Highway.
  • From Waikīkī: about 35 to 45 minutes by car on the Likelike or Pali Highway.
  • Allow extra time for traffic.

Rental cars

A car is the easiest way to get to the windward side and around the island. Book it early, since Labor Day weekend is busy. If you’re using a rideshare instead, plan your ride home ahead of time: pickups on the windward side at night can take longer than in Honolulu, and everyone has to leave the park by 10:30 pm.

Where to stay

Most hotels are in Waikīkī, about 35 to 45 minutes from the venue. Kāneʻohe and Kailua are closer but have few hotels.

Can we throw rice, petals, or confetti?

Please don’t. Park rules don’t allow confetti, glitter, rice, or artificial flower petals. Sparklers, candles, and balloon or lantern releases aren’t allowed either.

Smoking and vaping are banned in all Hawaiʻi State Parks. The venue provides one designated smoking area for events: [TBD] where it is.

Park rules allow alcohol only in and right around the event buildings. Open containers aren’t allowed anywhere else in the park, including the parking lot.
